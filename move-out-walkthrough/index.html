A move-out walkthrough that holds up

The cleanest deposit disputes are the ones with dated pictures. Spend an hour on this and it can save weeks.

Before the movers arrive

  • Find your move-in condition report or photos. Any pre-existing damage is the first thing to match against.
  • Ask the landlord in writing whether it offers a pre-move-out inspection and what it expects in terms of cleaning.
  • Patch only what your lease allows. Large nail holes, wall paint colors and carpet stains are lease-specific.

The walkthrough routine

  1. Empty every room. Take photos last, with all lights on.
  2. Shoot each wall, floor, ceiling and window; then close-ups of any marks. Use the phone's date stamp or email the photos to yourself.
  3. Open every cabinet, drawer, closet and the oven, fridge and dishwasher.
  4. Record a short video turning slowly through each room.
  5. Photograph meter readings if utilities are in your name.
  6. Return the keys and get written proof, like a signed receipt or an email reply.

Know the difference

Normal wear and tearLikely chargeable
Faded paint, minor carpet traffic marks, tiny nail holes from picturesLarge wall holes, pet damage, burns, broken fixtures, unpaid cleaning you agreed to

The table is our shorthand. The statute only says a landlord cannot keep money for normal wear and tear; what counts is decided case by case, and the lease may say more.

After you hand over the key

  • Email the landlord your forwarding address and the key-return date.
  • Calendar the one-month date; see the deposit law page.
  • Keep photos, texts and receipts until the deposit is resolved.

Moving-day damage that is not yours

If a mover scuffs a wall or an elevator, tell the building the same day. Your contract with the mover, not the landlord, covers that repair, and photographs taken before the crew arrived help show what was already there.
