Packing a small place without renting a big truck

When your whole life is under 200 square feet of closet, how you pack decides whether you need one helper or four.

Cull first, pack second

  • Sort into keep, sell, donate and trash on the same weekend. A decision made late costs money to haul.
  • Furniture that is cheap to replace is often cheaper to leave than to move.

Box sizes that work

BoxFill it with
SmallBooks, tools, canned food, anything dense
MediumKitchen items, shoes, small appliances
LargeBedding, pillows, lamp shades, light clothing

Heavy goes in small boxes. A box you can't comfortably carry up a stair is too heavy.

Stay ahead of the stairs

  • Break down bed frames and tables the night before and bag the hardware to the piece it belongs to.
  • Keep a "first night" bag: bedding, toiletries, chargers, a lamp.
  • Label the room each box belongs in on two sides.

Protect the floors

Use runners or flattened cardboard on hard floors and carpet, and use furniture sliders instead of dragging. In a rental, any scrape you cause is a deposit risk.

What to do with the leftovers

Denver's Large Item Pickup takes furniture, rugs and mattresses and box springs (bagged and wrapped) every nine weeks, up to 5 large items and 10 bags per address, set out by 6 a.m., and only on your scheduled pickup day. It does not take electronics or appliances. If you live in a multi-unit building, ask whether the building or the city services your trash before assuming that applies to you.

When a mover makes sense

If the whole place fits in a van and you only need a pair of hands, ask for a small-move or labor-only quote; the form on the home page asks about that.
