High-rise and condo moves in Denver

A tower has one freight elevator and one loading dock. Everything else about the day depends on those two.

Every building is different. The items below are the usual questions, not city or state rules. Your lease, HOA documents or building manager decide.

Ask the front desk

  • Is there a move-in or move-out fee or refundable deposit, and who pays it?
  • How long can I reserve the freight elevator, and how far ahead?
  • What hours and days are moves allowed?
  • Does the mover need to send a certificate of insurance, and what wording is required?
  • Where can the truck stand, and what is the clearance height?

The certificate of insurance step

Many buildings want a certificate of insurance from the mover before they confirm your elevator slot. The mover generally has to request it from its insurer, so ask for the building's sample wording early and pass it along when you book.

Streets and curbs around the building

If the truck can't use a dock and has to take street space, Denver's transportation department handles Street Occupancy permits and meter bagging requests through its E-Permits system. Its page does not mention moving trucks specifically, and bagging requests there take up to 72 hours to process, so ask the city and your building about plans early.

Protect the common areas

  • Pads in the elevator and runners in the hall, if the building supplies them or requires them.
  • Photos of the hallway and elevator walls before and after.
  • A single point of contact on moving day who can speak to the front desk.

Altitude and stairs

If the elevator fails, you may be carrying up several flights at roughly 5,280 feet above sea level. Plan breaks and water for your helpers and tell any hired crew about the stairs before you book so the estimate reflects it.

If you want a mover for a high-rise, say so on the form, so the request goes to crews who will handle the elevator booking with you.

Sources (read 11 Nov 2026)

  • Building rules are set by each property; there is no public rulebook to cite.
